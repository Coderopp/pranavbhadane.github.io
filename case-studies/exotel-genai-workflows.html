From Organizational Discovery to GenAI-Powered Business Workflows

Exotel — connecting customer intelligence with product knowledge through a Salesforce-integrated RAG assistant.

Role: Strategy & Finance InternJune–August 202480+ employee rollout

01. Start with the business, not the model

Exotel's enterprise communications portfolio spans CPaaS, CCaaS, Voice APIs, chatbots, WhatsApp bots and Conversational AI. Documentation established product capabilities; it did not reveal how teams actually made decisions or where information access broke down.

I worked with Product Managers and conducted 30+ one-to-one conversations across Sales, Customer Happiness and Product to map the daily workflows behind customer engagement, product discovery and upsell identification.

The interviews asked how teams discovered customer requirements, accessed product information, handled customer queries and identified cross-sell opportunities—not simply what features they wanted.

03. The opportunity: product knowledge + customer context

Sales teams needed to understand a customer's existing product usage while navigating a broad portfolio of potential offerings. Employees also needed a faster way to find accurate product information. The core opportunity was to extend an existing RAG chatbot rather than build a parallel conversational AI stack.

Customer intelligence

Use Salesforce CRM context to help Sales understand current product usage and consider relevant additional offerings during customer interactions.

Product intelligence

Give employees conversational access to Exotel's existing product knowledge base without searching across multiple sources.

04. Salesforce integration

The assistant joined two information sources: Exotel's existing product knowledge and customer-specific information from Salesforce CRM. This is a conceptual workflow representation, not a claim about the exact internal implementation topology.

05. From opportunity to rollout

Implementation required alignment with Sales leadership, Customer Happiness leadership, Product Managers and function VPs. The process validated the problem, prioritized requirements and evaluated whether CRM connectivity, existing conversational capabilities and available customer context could support the workflow.

Technical feasibility

  • Integration with existing Exotel products
  • CRM connectivity and customer context availability
  • Reuse of existing conversational AI capabilities

Economic feasibility

  • Development and integration costs
  • Inference, token and operating costs
  • Expected productivity benefits

The resulting assistant was rolled out to more than 80 employees, supporting both customer intelligence and conversational product retrieval.

06. Commercial strategy alongside implementation

In parallel, I analyzed Conversational AI pricing: competitor positioning, packaging and pricing structures, financials, subscription opportunities and tiered usage limits. The work identified three pricing-related growth opportunities with an estimated ~7% revenue uplift.

This is a projected opportunity, not a verified realized revenue increase.

07. What this demonstrated

This project connected discovery, execution and business judgment: uncovering workflow problems through research; translating them into a technically feasible, CRM-integrated GenAI solution; and aligning costs, stakeholders and commercial value. The outcome was not a generic chatbot, but an attempt to fit conversational AI into the way an enterprise actually operates.
